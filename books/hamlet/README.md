# Hamlet, Prince of Denmark — Modern English Edition

**William Shakespeare, c. 1601** · rendered into modern English, 2026

An act-by-act modern-English rendering of *Hamlet* produced for readers
working through the Great Books of Western Thought. This is a full translation
of the play, not a synopsis: every speech, song, letter, and stage direction
is rendered, scene by scene, with speakers labeled. Each act file contains an
**Overview**, **Key Concepts** (the themes that act develops), an **Archaic
Terms** glossary (including lost puns), and the complete **Modern
Translation**. Iconic lines that are still fully intelligible ("To be, or not
to be — that is the question") are kept verbatim.

**Source:** [Project Gutenberg #1524](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1524)
(Collins edition), preserved unmodernized at [`source/1524.txt`](source/1524.txt).
The machine-readable index is [`book.json`](book.json).

## Why this play matters

*Hamlet* is the pivot of Western drama: a revenge tragedy that turns inward,
where the obstacle is not the villain but the hero's own thinking. Its great
questions — how we can act under uncertainty, whether conscience makes cowards
of us, what a person is when "seeming" and being come apart, what we owe the
dead — connect backward to Greek tragedy and the Bible and forward to
Montaigne (whom Shakespeare was reading), Descartes's doubt, and modern
psychology. It is also the most quoted secular text in English; half its
lines have become the language itself.

## Translation notes

Verse and prose are both rendered as clear modern prose (songs as short free
verse), keeping each character's voice — Hamlet's wit, Polonius's windiness,
the gravediggers' clowning. Puns are translated so the joke lands; where a
double meaning can't survive, the lost sense is recorded in that act's
Archaic Terms table. Stage directions appear in italic brackets. Nothing is
cut: the player speeches, the songs, and the letters are all here.
