# Act IV

> William Shakespeare, *Hamlet, Prince of Denmark* (c. 1601) · Act IV, Scenes 1–7
>
> [← Act III](03-act-iii.md) · [Book index](../README.md) · [Act V →](05-act-v.md)

## Overview

Act IV is the play's great unraveling. The killing of Polonius at the end of Act III detonates through every household in Elsinore, and this act tracks the shrapnel. Claudius, now certain that Hamlet is lethally dangerous, hustles him onto a ship for England — with sealed letters ordering his immediate execution on arrival. Hamlet goes almost cheerfully, taunting the King with grim jokes about worms eating Polonius and kings passing through the guts of beggars. On the way to the coast he sees Fortinbras's Norwegian army marching to die over a worthless scrap of Poland, and in his last great soliloquy ("How all occasions do inform against me") he shames himself with the comparison: twenty thousand men will die for an eggshell of honor while he, with a murdered father and every reason to act, still delays. He resolves that from now on his thoughts will be bloody or worthless.

Back at the castle, the cost of Hamlet's deed falls on the innocent. Ophelia, her father killed by the man she loved and buried in unseemly haste, goes genuinely mad — and her madness, unlike Hamlet's performed version, is unbearable to watch: she drifts through the court singing fragments of songs about dead old men and betrayed girls, handing out flowers loaded with meanings no one wants to receive. Her brother Laertes storms back from France at the head of a mob ready to make him king, demanding vengeance for his father — a dark mirror of Hamlet, a son who does not delay for a single second.

Claudius, at his most coolly brilliant, absorbs Laertes's rebellion and redirects it. When word arrives that Hamlet has escaped the England trap (rescued by pirates, of all things) and is heading home, the King converts Laertes's rage into a murder weapon: a rigged fencing match with an unblunted sword, which Laertes improves with poison on the blade, and which Claudius backs up with a poisoned cup of wine. The act closes with the Queen's famous, strangely beautiful report that Ophelia has drowned — garlanded with flowers, singing as the water pulled her under. Every mechanism of the catastrophe of Act V is now armed and set.

## Key Concepts

- **Revenge and delay** — the act sets three avenging sons side by side. Fortinbras marches an army over a worthless plot of ground for honor's sake; Laertes bursts through the palace doors within days of his father's death, ready to cut Hamlet's throat in a church; and Hamlet, watching Fortinbras's soldiers, indicts his own inaction and vows bloody thoughts. Laertes's instant, unreflecting revenge shows exactly what Hamlet is not — and how easily a pure avenger can be manipulated.
- **Madness real and feigned** — Ophelia's genuine breakdown arrives as the answer to Hamlet's "antic disposition." Her songs and flowers are shapeless, yet listeners "botch the words up" to fit their own suspicions: real madness turns out to be politically dangerous in a way performed madness never was.
- **The corruption spreading from the throne** — Claudius's imagery is all disease: Hamlet is a "hectic" fever in his blood, desperate diseases need desperate remedies, Denmark's grief is an ulcer. The hidden fratricide now infects everyone — Gertrude's "sick soul," the "muddied" mob, the poisoned sword and cup.
- **Kingship, legitimacy, and the mob** — the crowd that acclaims "Laertes shall be king!" exposes how thin Claudius's legitimacy is; he cannot even prosecute Hamlet openly because the "distracted multitude" loves him. Yet Claudius also invokes the "divinity" that hedges a king — from the mouth of a king-killer, pure irony.
- **The body and death as the great leveler** — Hamlet's worm-and-beggar riddles reduce kingship to a dish at the worms' table: fat king and lean beggar are "two dishes, but to one table." The theme flowers fully in the graveyard of Act V, but it is planted here, aimed straight at Claudius.
- **Words, letters, and plots** — the act runs on documents: Claudius's sealed death-warrant to England, Hamlet's letters to Horatio and the King announcing his "naked" return, the whispered slanders Claudius fears. Plots are drafted, redrafted, and given a "back or second" — the poisoned cup — in case the first draft fails.
- **Providence and accident** — pirates rescue Hamlet; a branch breaks under Ophelia. The plot begins to turn on events no one designed, preparing Act V's meditation on the divinity that shapes our ends.

## Modern Translation

### Act IV, Scene 1

> *Scene: A room in the Castle.*

*[Enter King, Queen, Rosencrantz and Guildenstern.]*

**KING CLAUDIUS:** There's meaning in these sighs — these deep heaves of yours. You must translate them; I need to understand. Where is your son?

**QUEEN GERTRUDE:** Give us this room for a little while.

*[To Rosencrantz and Guildenstern, who go out.]*

Oh, my good lord — what I have seen tonight!

**KING CLAUDIUS:** What, Gertrude? How is Hamlet?

**QUEEN GERTRUDE:** Mad as the sea and the wind when the two of them fight over which is stronger. In one of his lawless fits, he heard something stir behind the tapestry, whipped out his sword, shouted "A rat! A rat!" — and in that deranged delusion he killed the good old man hiding there, sight unseen.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Oh, what a terrible deed! It would have been me, if I'd been standing there. His being loose is a threat to everyone — to you, to me, to us all. God, how are we going to answer for this bloody act? The blame will land on me: my foresight should have kept this mad young man on a short leash, restrained, away from other people. But I loved him so much I refused to see what was clearly necessary — like a man with a foul disease who, to keep it from becoming known, lets it eat away at the very marrow of his life. Where has he gone?

**QUEEN GERTRUDE:** To drag away the body he killed. And over that body, his madness shows itself pure — like a vein of gold in a lode of base metals: he's weeping for what he's done.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Oh, Gertrude, come away! The sun will no sooner touch the mountaintops than we'll have him shipped out of here. As for this vile deed — we'll have to use all our royal authority and all our skill both to own it and to explain it away. — Guildenstern!

*[Re-enter Rosencrantz and Guildenstern.]*

Friends — both of you — go get some more help. Hamlet, in his madness, has killed Polonius and dragged the body out of his mother's room. Go find him. Speak gently to him, and bring the body into the chapel. Please hurry.

*[Exeunt Rosencrantz and Guildenstern.]*

Come, Gertrude. We'll summon our wisest friends and tell them both what we intend to do and what has so unfortunately been done. That way slander — whose whisper carries its poisoned shot straight across the diameter of the world, as level as a cannonball to the bull's-eye — may miss our name and hit only the invulnerable air. Oh, come away! My soul is full of discord and dismay.

*[Exeunt.]*

### Act IV, Scene 2

> *Scene: Another room in the Castle.*

*[Enter Hamlet.]*

**HAMLET:** Safely stowed.

**ROSENCRANTZ and GUILDENSTERN:** *[Offstage.]* Hamlet! Lord Hamlet!

**HAMLET:** What's that noise? Who's calling for Hamlet? Ah — here they come.

*[Enter Rosencrantz and Guildenstern.]*

**ROSENCRANTZ:** What have you done with the dead body, my lord?

**HAMLET:** Mixed it with the dust — they're relatives, after all.

**ROSENCRANTZ:** Tell us where it is, so we can take it from there and carry it to the chapel.

**HAMLET:** Don't believe it.

**ROSENCRANTZ:** Believe what?

**HAMLET:** That I can keep your secrets and not my own. Besides — to be interrogated by a *sponge*! What kind of answer is the son of a king supposed to give to that?

**ROSENCRANTZ:** You take me for a sponge, my lord?

**HAMLET:** Yes, sir — one that soaks up the King's favor, his rewards, his positions of authority. But officers like you actually serve the king best at the end: he keeps you the way an ape keeps food in the corner of its jaw — first into the mouth, last to be swallowed. When he needs what you've soaked up, he just squeezes you — and, sponge, you'll be dry again.

**ROSENCRANTZ:** I don't understand you, my lord.

**HAMLET:** I'm glad of it. A sly speech sleeps soundly in a foolish ear.

**ROSENCRANTZ:** My lord, you must tell us where the body is, and come with us to the King.

**HAMLET:** The body is with the King, but the King is not with the body. The King is a thing —

**GUILDENSTERN:** A "thing," my lord!

**HAMLET:** — of nothing. Take me to him. Hide, fox, and everyone chase after!

*[Exeunt.]*

### Act IV, Scene 3

> *Scene: Another room in the Castle.*

*[Enter King, attended.]*

**KING CLAUDIUS:** I've sent men to find him, and to find the body. How dangerous it is to have this man on the loose! And yet I can't bring the full weight of the law down on him: he's loved by the confused masses, who judge with their eyes, not their reason — and where that's true, people weigh how harsh the offender's punishment is, never the offense itself. To keep everything looking smooth and calm, this sudden decision to ship him off has to look like careful deliberation. Diseases grown desperate are cured by desperate remedies — or not at all.

*[Enter Rosencrantz.]*

Well? What's happened?

**ROSENCRANTZ:** My lord, we cannot get out of him where the dead body is stowed.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** But where is *he*?

**ROSENCRANTZ:** Outside, my lord — under guard, awaiting your orders.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Bring him before us.

**ROSENCRANTZ:** Guildenstern! Bring in my lord.

*[Enter Hamlet and Guildenstern.]*

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Now, Hamlet — where's Polonius?

**HAMLET:** At supper.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** At supper! Where?

**HAMLET:** Not where he's eating, but where he's being eaten. A certain convocation of politic worms is at him right now. Your worm is the one true emperor of the dinner table: we fatten up every other creature to fatten ourselves, and we fatten ourselves for the maggots. Your fat king and your lean beggar are just two courses on the same menu — two dishes, one table. That's how it ends.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Alas, alas!

**HAMLET:** A man can fish with the worm that has eaten of a king, and then eat the fish that fed on that worm.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** What do you mean by this?

**HAMLET:** Nothing — just to show you how a king can make a royal progress through the guts of a beggar.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Where is Polonius?

**HAMLET:** In heaven. Send someone up to check. If your messenger doesn't find him there, go look in the other place yourself. But honestly — if you haven't found him within the month, you'll smell him as you go up the stairs into the lobby.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** *[To some Attendants.]* Go look for him there.

**HAMLET:** He'll wait till you come.

*[Exeunt Attendants.]*

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Hamlet, this deed — for your own personal safety, which matters to us as dearly as we grieve for what you've done — requires that we send you away from here with fiery speed. So get yourself ready. The ship is waiting, the wind is favorable, your companions are standing by, and everything is set — for England.

**HAMLET:** For England!

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Yes, Hamlet.

**HAMLET:** Good.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** It is good — if you knew our purposes.

**HAMLET:** I see a cherub who sees them. But come on — to England! Farewell, dear mother.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Your loving *father*, Hamlet.

**HAMLET:** My mother. Father and mother are man and wife; man and wife are one flesh; therefore — my mother. Come on: England!

*[Exit.]*

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Stay right on his heels. Coax him aboard quickly — no delays. I want him gone tonight. Go! Everything else touching this business is sealed and finished. Please — hurry.

*[Exeunt Rosencrantz and Guildenstern.]*

And you, England — if you value my friendship at all (and my great power should teach you to value it, since the scar from the Danish sword still looks raw and red on you, and your uncoerced awe pays homage to us) — you will not treat coldly our royal command, which requires in full, by letters urging exactly this, the immediate death of Hamlet. Do it, England. He rages in my blood like a fever, and you must cure me. Until I know it's done, whatever my fortunes, my joys will never begin.

*[Exit.]*

### Act IV, Scene 4

> *Scene: A plain in Denmark.*

*[Enter Fortinbras, and Forces marching.]*

**FORTINBRAS:** Go, Captain. Greet the Danish king from me. Tell him that, with his permission, Fortinbras requests the escort for the march across his kingdom that was promised. You know the rendezvous point. If his majesty wants anything from us, we'll pay our respects to him in person — tell him that.

**CAPTAIN:** I will, my lord.

**FORTINBRAS:** Move out — slow march.

*[Exeunt Fortinbras and Forces.]*

*[Enter Hamlet, Rosencrantz, Guildenstern, and others.]*

**HAMLET:** Good sir, whose troops are these?

**CAPTAIN:** Norway's, sir.

**HAMLET:** And their objective, sir, if I may ask?

**CAPTAIN:** They march against some part of Poland.

**HAMLET:** Who commands them, sir?

**CAPTAIN:** Old Norway's nephew — Fortinbras.

**HAMLET:** Is this aimed at the Polish heartland, sir, or at some border territory?

**CAPTAIN:** To tell you the truth, without exaggeration: we're going to take a little patch of ground that has nothing of value in it but the name. I wouldn't pay five ducats — five — to farm it; and it wouldn't bring Norway or Poland a penny more if it were sold outright.

**HAMLET:** Well then, the Poles will never bother defending it.

**CAPTAIN:** Oh yes — it's already garrisoned.

**HAMLET:** Two thousand souls and twenty thousand ducats won't settle the argument over this straw. This is the abscess of too much wealth and peace — it bursts inward, and shows no outward sign of why the man dies. — I humbly thank you, sir.

**CAPTAIN:** God be with you, sir.

*[Exit.]*

**ROSENCRANTZ:** Will you come along now, my lord?

**HAMLET:** I'll catch up with you shortly. Go on ahead a little.

*[Exeunt all but Hamlet.]*

How everything I see accuses me and spurs on my sluggish revenge! What is a man, if the best use and profit of his time is nothing but sleeping and feeding? A beast — nothing more. Surely whoever made us with such wide-ranging minds, able to look before and after, didn't give us that capacity, that godlike reason, to grow moldy in us unused. Now — whether it's animal forgetfulness, or some cowardly scruple of thinking too precisely about the outcome (a kind of thinking that, cut in quarters, is one part wisdom and three parts coward, always) — I honestly don't know why I'm still alive to say "This thing is still to do," since I have the cause, and the will, and the strength, and the means to do it. Examples as huge and obvious as the earth itself urge me on. Look at this army — this mass, this expense — led by a delicate and tender prince whose spirit, puffed up with divine ambition, makes faces at the invisible outcome, exposing everything mortal and uncertain in him to whatever fortune, death, and danger dare to do — and for an eggshell. True greatness isn't refusing to act without a great cause; it's finding cause for a great quarrel in a straw, when honor is at stake. So where does that leave me — with a father murdered, a mother defiled, provocations enough for my reason and my blood — and I let it all sleep? While, to my shame, I watch the imminent deaths of twenty thousand men who, for a fantasy and an illusion of fame, go to their graves as if going to bed — fighting for a plot of ground too small for their armies even to contest it on, not tomb enough, not ground enough, to hide the dead? Oh — from this moment on, my thoughts be bloody, or be nothing worth!

*[Exit.]*

### Act IV, Scene 5

> *Scene: Elsinore. A room in the Castle.*

*[Enter Queen and Horatio.]*

**QUEEN GERTRUDE:** I will not speak with her.

**GENTLEMAN:** She's insistent — truly out of her mind. Her condition deserves pity.

**QUEEN GERTRUDE:** What does she want?

**GENTLEMAN:** She talks constantly about her father; says she hears there are tricks being played in the world; and she coughs, and beats at her breast, and kicks spitefully at trifles, and says things half in doubt that carry only half their sense. Her speech is nothing — and yet the shapelessness of it moves everyone who hears it to try to piece it together. They guess at it, and patch her words together to fit their own thoughts; and the way she winks and nods and gestures as she speaks would really make you think there might be meaning in it — nothing certain, but something very unhappy. It would be wise to speak with her; she could sow dangerous speculations in malicious minds.

**QUEEN GERTRUDE:** Let her come in.

*[Exit Horatio.]*

*(Aside.)* To my sick soul — and that's sin's true nature — every trifle looks like the prologue to some great disaster. Guilt is so full of clumsy suspicion, it spills itself in its very fear of being spilled.

*[Re-enter Horatio with Ophelia.]*

**OPHELIA:** Where is the beautiful majesty of Denmark?

**QUEEN GERTRUDE:** How are you, Ophelia?

**OPHELIA:** *[Sings.]*

> How should I know your true love
> from any other one?
> By his cockle hat and staff
> and his sandal shoes.

**QUEEN GERTRUDE:** Oh, sweet lady — what does this song mean?

**OPHELIA:** What did you say? No — please, just listen.

*[Sings.]*

> He is dead and gone, lady,
> he is dead and gone;
> at his head a grass-green turf,
> at his heels a stone.

**QUEEN GERTRUDE:** No, but Ophelia —

**OPHELIA:** Please. Listen.

*[Sings.]*

> White his shroud as the mountain snow —

*[Enter King.]*

**QUEEN GERTRUDE:** Oh — look at this, my lord!

**OPHELIA:** *[Sings.]*

> — strewn all over with sweet flowers;
> which went to the grave unwatered
> by true-love's showers.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** How are you, pretty lady?

**OPHELIA:** Well — God reward you! They say the owl was a baker's daughter. Lord, we know what we are, but we don't know what we may become. God be at your table!

**KING CLAUDIUS:** She's brooding on her father.

**OPHELIA:** Please, let's have no words about this; but when they ask you what it means, tell them this:

*[Sings.]*

> Tomorrow is Saint Valentine's day,
> all in the morning early,
> and I a maid at your window,
> to be your Valentine.
>
> Then up he rose and donned his clothes
> and opened the chamber door;
> let in the maid, that out a maid
> never departed more.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Pretty Ophelia!

**OPHELIA:** Truly — and without swearing to it, I'll finish the song:

*[Sings.]*

> By Jesus and by Saint Charity,
> alack, and fie for shame!
> Young men will do it if they get the chance;
> by God, they are to blame.
>
> "Before you tumbled me," said she,
> "you promised me to wed."
> "And so I would have, by that sun there,
> if you hadn't come to my bed."

**KING CLAUDIUS:** How long has she been like this?

**OPHELIA:** I hope all will be well. We must be patient. But I can't help weeping to think they'd lay him in the cold ground. My brother shall hear of it. And so — thank you for your good advice. Come, my coach! Good night, ladies. Good night, sweet ladies. Good night, good night.

*[Exit.]*

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Follow her closely. Keep a careful watch on her, please.

*[Exit Horatio.]*

Oh, this is the poison of deep grief — it all springs from her father's death. Oh Gertrude, Gertrude — when sorrows come, they come not single spies, but in battalions! First, her father killed. Next, your son gone — and he himself the violent author of his own well-deserved banishment. The common people muddied — thick and unwholesome in their thoughts and whispers about good Polonius's death — and we acted like amateurs, burying him in secret haste. Poor Ophelia, divided from herself and from her clear judgment — and without judgment we're just pictures, or mere animals. And last, and carrying as much weight as all the rest together: her brother has come back secretly from France. He feeds on his own bewilderment, keeps himself hidden in clouds, and there's no shortage of gossips buzzing in his ear with poisonous stories about his father's death — stories in which necessity, being starved of facts, won't hesitate to accuse me personally, from ear to ear. Oh, my dear Gertrude — all this, like an artillery piece loaded with scattershot, is killing me many times over.

*[A noise within.]*

**QUEEN GERTRUDE:** Oh no — what noise is that?

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Where are my Swiss guards? Have them hold the door.

*[Enter a Gentleman.]*

What is the matter?

**GENTLEMAN:** Save yourself, my lord! The ocean, overflowing its bounds, doesn't devour the lowlands with more furious haste than young Laertes, at the head of a rioting mob, is overwhelming your officers. The rabble are calling him "lord"; and — as if the world were only now beginning, as if all tradition were forgotten and custom unknown, the very things that ratify and prop up every claim we make — they're shouting "Let us choose! Laertes shall be king!" Caps, hands, and tongues applaud it to the clouds: "Laertes shall be king! Laertes king!"

**QUEEN GERTRUDE:** How cheerfully they bay on the false trail! You're tracking it backwards, you false Danish dogs!

*[A noise within.]*

**KING CLAUDIUS:** The doors are broken down.

*[Enter Laertes, armed; Danes following.]*

**LAERTES:** Where is this king? — Sirs, all of you, wait outside.

**DANES:** No — let us come in!

**LAERTES:** Please — leave this to me.

**DANES:** We will, we will.

*[They retire without the door.]*

**LAERTES:** Thank you. Guard the door. — You vile king: give me my father!

**QUEEN GERTRUDE:** Calmly, good Laertes.

**LAERTES:** Any drop of blood in me that stays calm proclaims me a bastard — shouts "cuckold" at my father, brands the word "whore" right here, on the chaste, unblemished brow of my true mother.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** What is the cause, Laertes, that makes your rebellion look so gigantic? — Let him go, Gertrude. Don't fear for my person: there's such divinity hedging a king that treason can only peep at what it wants and act out almost none of its will. — Tell me, Laertes, why you're so enraged. — Let him go, Gertrude. — Speak, man.

**LAERTES:** Where is my father?

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Dead.

**QUEEN GERTRUDE:** But not by him.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Let him ask everything he wants.

**LAERTES:** How did he die? I won't be played with. To hell with allegiance! Vows — to the blackest devil! Conscience and grace — to the deepest pit! I dare damnation. This is where I stand: both worlds, this one and the next, I throw away; let come what comes — only I *will* be revenged, most thoroughly, for my father.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Who's going to stop you?

**LAERTES:** My own will — and nothing else in the whole world. And as for my means, I'll manage them so well that a little will go a very long way.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Good Laertes: if you want to know the truth about your dear father's death — is it written into your revenge that, like a gambler sweeping the whole table, you'll take friend and foe alike, winner and loser?

**LAERTES:** None but his enemies.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Then do you want to know who they are?

**LAERTES:** To his good friends I'll open my arms this wide — and, like the life-giving pelican, feed them with my own blood.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Ah — now you speak like a good child and a true gentleman. That I am guiltless of your father's death, and grieve for it most deeply, will pierce as straight into your judgment as daylight into your eye.

**DANES:** *[Within.]* Let her come in!

**LAERTES:** What now? What's that noise?

*[Re-enter Ophelia, fantastically dressed with straws and flowers.]*

O heat, dry up my brains! Tears seven times salt, burn out the sense and power of my eyes! — By heaven, your madness will be paid back by weight, until our side of the scale crashes down. O rose of May! Dear girl, kind sister, sweet Ophelia! — O heavens! Is it possible a young girl's sanity should be as mortal as an old man's life? Nature is refined by love; and where it's refined, it sends some precious part of itself after the thing it loves.

**OPHELIA:** *[Sings.]*

> They carried him bare-faced on the bier
> — hey non nonny, nonny, hey nonny —
> and on his grave rained many a tear.

Fare you well, my dove!

**LAERTES:** If you had your wits and were arguing for revenge, you couldn't be half so persuasive as this.

**OPHELIA:** You must sing "Down a-down," and you, "call him a-down-a." Oh, how well the refrain suits it! It's about the false steward who stole his master's daughter.

**LAERTES:** This nonsense says more than sense ever could.

**OPHELIA:** There's rosemary — that's for remembrance. Please, love, remember. And there are pansies — they're for thoughts.

**LAERTES:** A lesson taught in madness: thoughts and remembrance, perfectly matched.

**OPHELIA:** There's fennel for you, and columbines. There's rue for you — and here's some for me; we can call it the herb of grace on Sundays. Oh — you must wear your rue with a difference. There's a daisy. I would give you some violets, but they all withered when my father died. They say he made a good end —

*[Sings.]*

> For bonny sweet Robin is all my joy —

**LAERTES:** Sorrow and affliction, suffering, hell itself — she turns them all into charm and prettiness.

**OPHELIA:** *[Sings.]*

> And will he not come again?
> And will he not come again?
> No, no, he is dead;
> go to your death-bed:
> he never will come again.
>
> His beard was as white as snow,
> all flaxen was his head.
> He is gone, he is gone,
> and we cast away moan:
> God have mercy on his soul!

And on all Christian souls, I pray God. God be with you.

*[Exit.]*

**LAERTES:** Do you see this, O God?

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Laertes, I must share in your grief — you have no right to deny me that. Just step aside with me. Choose whichever of your wisest friends you like, and they shall hear the case and judge between you and me. If they find me implicated, directly or indirectly, we will hand over our kingdom, our crown, our life, and everything we call ours to you in payment. But if not, then be content to lend me your patience, and I will work together with your soul to give it the satisfaction it's owed.

**LAERTES:** Let it be so. The manner of his death, his furtive burial — no memorial trophy, no sword, no coat of arms hung over his bones, no noble rite, no formal ceremony — all of it cries out to be heard, as if from heaven to earth, and I must call it to account.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** So you shall — and wherever the offense lies, let the great axe fall. Please, come with me.

*[Exeunt.]*

### Act IV, Scene 6

> *Scene: Another room in the Castle.*

*[Enter Horatio and a Servant.]*

**HORATIO:** Who are these people who want to speak with me?

**SERVANT:** Sailors, sir. They say they have letters for you.

**HORATIO:** Show them in.

*[Exit Servant.]*

I can't imagine what part of the world would send me greetings — unless it's from Lord Hamlet.

*[Enter Sailors.]*

**FIRST SAILOR:** God bless you, sir.

**HORATIO:** And you too.

**FIRST SAILOR:** He will, sir, if it pleases him. There's a letter for you, sir — it comes from the ambassador who was bound for England — if your name is Horatio, as I've been given to understand it is.

**HORATIO:** *[Reads.]* "Horatio, when you have looked this over, arrange for these fellows to get access to the King: they have letters for him. Before we were two days old at sea, a pirate ship rigged for serious fighting gave us chase. Finding ourselves too slow under sail, we put on a courage we didn't feel; and in the grappling, I boarded them. In that instant they broke free of our ship, so I alone became their prisoner. They have treated me like merciful thieves — but they knew what they were doing: I'm to do them a good turn in exchange. See that the King gets the letters I've sent, and then come to me as fast as you would flee from death. I have words to speak in your ear that will strike you dumb — and even they are far too light a caliber for the size of the matter. These good fellows will bring you to where I am. Rosencrantz and Guildenstern are still on course for England; about them I have much to tell you. Farewell. He that you know is yours, HAMLET."

Come — I'll clear the way for you to deliver these letters of yours, and all the quicker so that you can then take me to the man you brought them from.

*[Exeunt.]*

### Act IV, Scene 7

> *Scene: Another room in the Castle.*

*[Enter King and Laertes.]*

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Now your conscience must seal my acquittal, and you must set me down in your heart as a friend — since you've heard, with an understanding ear, that the man who killed your noble father was aiming at my life.

**LAERTES:** So it appears. But tell me why you took no action against acts so criminal, so punishable by death — when your own safety, your wisdom, everything else, must have urged you so powerfully to do it.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Oh — for two particular reasons, which may seem feeble to you, perhaps, but to me they are strong. The Queen his mother practically lives on the sight of him; and as for me — call it my virtue or my curse, whichever it is — she is so bound up with my life and soul that, just as a star can't move outside its own sphere, I can't move except by her. The other reason I couldn't take it to a public trial is the great love the common people bear him. Dipping all his faults in their affection, they'd be like the spring that turns wood to stone — they'd convert his shackles into badges of honor. So my arrows, too lightly shafted for so loud a wind, would have flown right back to my bow instead of hitting where I aimed them.

**LAERTES:** And so I have lost a noble father; had a sister driven into desperate madness — a sister whose worth, if praise can look backward at what she was, stood on the mountaintop, challenging the whole age to match her perfections. But my revenge will come.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Don't lose sleep over that. You mustn't think I'm made of stuff so flat and dull that I can let danger grab me by the beard and consider it a game. You'll hear more shortly. I loved your father, and we love ourself — and that, I hope, will teach you to imagine —

*[Enter a Messenger.]*

Yes? What news?

**MESSENGER:** Letters, my lord — from Hamlet. This one for your majesty; this one for the Queen.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** From Hamlet! Who brought them?

**MESSENGER:** Sailors, my lord, they say; I didn't see them. They were given to me by Claudio — he received them from the man who brought them.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Laertes, you shall hear them. — Leave us.

*[Exit Messenger.]*

*[Reads.]* "High and mighty: You shall know that I have been set down naked in your kingdom. Tomorrow I shall beg leave to see your kingly eyes; and then — after first asking your pardon for it — I shall recount the circumstances of my sudden, and stranger, return. HAMLET."

What can this mean? Have all the others come back too? Or is it some hoax, and nothing of the kind?

**LAERTES:** Do you know the handwriting?

**KING CLAUDIUS:** It's Hamlet's hand. "Naked"! And in a postscript here he adds: "alone." Can you make sense of it for me?

**LAERTES:** I'm lost in it, my lord. But let him come. It warms the very sickness in my heart that I'll live to look him in the teeth and say, "This is what you did."

**KING CLAUDIUS:** If this is real, Laertes — and how can it be? and yet how can it be otherwise? — will you let yourself be guided by me?

**LAERTES:** Yes, my lord — as long as you don't guide me toward making peace.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Toward your own peace. If he has indeed come back — balking at his voyage and meaning never to resume it — I'll steer him into an exploit, now ripe in my planning, in which he cannot help but fall. And for his death, not a breath of blame will stir; even his mother will acquit the scheme and call it an accident.

**LAERTES:** My lord, I'll be guided by you — all the more gladly if you can arrange it so that I'm the instrument.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** It falls out perfectly. Since your travels, you've been much talked about — and in Hamlet's hearing — for a skill in which they say you shine. The whole sum of your accomplishments didn't wring as much envy out of him as that one alone did — and it's the one that, in my view, ranks lowest.

**LAERTES:** What skill is that, my lord?

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Nothing but a ribbon in the cap of youth — yet a necessary one; for light, carefree finery becomes the young no less than furs and sober gowns become the old, denoting prosperity and dignity. Two months ago there was a gentleman of Normandy here. I've seen the French myself — campaigned against them — and they ride well; but this gallant had witchcraft in it. He grew into his saddle, and brought his horse to such wondrous performances that he seemed to share one body, one half-nature, with the splendid beast. He so far surpassed anything I could imagine that I, inventing feats and maneuvers in my head, fell short of what he actually did.

**LAERTES:** A Norman, was he?

**KING CLAUDIUS:** A Norman.

**LAERTES:** On my life — Lamond.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** The very man.

**LAERTES:** I know him well. He's the brooch, the jewel, of his whole nation.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** He gave a testimonial about *you* — and such a masterly report of your art and practice in fencing, your rapier work above all, that he cried out it would be a sight worth seeing if anyone could match you. He swore the swordsmen of his own nation had neither attack, nor guard, nor eye when you faced them. Sir, this report of his so envenomed Hamlet with envy that he could do nothing but wish and beg for you to come back quickly and fence with him. Now — out of this —

**LAERTES:** Out of this — what, my lord?

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Laertes, was your father dear to you? Or are you like a painting of sorrow — a face without a heart?

**LAERTES:** Why do you ask that?

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Not because I think you didn't love your father — but because I know that love is kindled by time, and I've seen, in real cases that prove it, that time dampens love's spark and fire. There lives within the very flame of love a kind of wick or snuff that will dim it; nothing keeps the same goodness forever, for goodness, grown to excess, dies of its own too-much. What we *would* do, we should do while we *would* — because this "would" changes, and suffers as many weakenings and delays as there are tongues, and hands, and accidents; and then this "should" becomes like a spendthrift's sigh — relief that costs you blood. But — to the raw center of the ulcer: Hamlet is coming back. What would you undertake, to show yourself your father's son in deeds rather than words?

**LAERTES:** To cut his throat in the church.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** True enough — no place should give murder sanctuary; revenge should have no boundaries. But, good Laertes, if you'll do this, keep to your room. When Hamlet gets home, he'll learn that you've returned. We'll set people on to praise your excellence and put a double coat of varnish on the reputation the Frenchman gave you — bring the two of you together at last, and lay bets on your heads. He, being careless, completely generous, and free of all scheming, won't inspect the foils; so with ease — or with a little sleight of hand — you can pick out a sword with no button on the point, and, in one treacherous thrust, repay him for your father.

**LAERTES:** I'll do it — and to that end, I'll anoint my sword. I bought a poison from a quack doctor, so deadly that if you merely dip a knife in it, wherever it draws blood no poultice, however rare — compounded from every healing herb under the moon — can save the thing that's even scratched by it. I'll touch my point with this contagion, so that if I so much as graze him, it may be death.

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Let's think about this further — weigh what timing and what means will best fit our design. If this should fail, and our intent show through our clumsy performance, it would be better never attempted. So this project needs a backup — a second plan that will hold if the first blows up in the testing. Wait — let me see. We'll lay a solemn wager on your respective skills… I have it! When the exercise makes you both hot and dry — make your bouts more violent precisely to that end — and he calls for a drink, I'll have prepared a chalice for the occasion: one sip, and if by chance he's escaped your poisoned thrust, our purpose holds there.

*[Enter Queen.]*

What is it, sweet queen?

**QUEEN GERTRUDE:** One sorrow treads on another's heel, they come so fast. Your sister's drowned, Laertes.

**LAERTES:** Drowned! Oh — where?

**QUEEN GERTRUDE:** There's a willow that grows aslant a brook, mirroring its silver-gray leaves in the glassy stream. She came there with fantastic garlands of crowflowers, nettles, daisies, and long purples — the ones free-spoken shepherds call by a coarser name, though our cold-blooded maids call them dead men's fingers. There, climbing out to hang her crown of weeds on the overhanging branches, a spiteful sliver of the bough broke; down came her flowery trophies and Ophelia with them, into the weeping brook. Her clothes spread wide, and for a while they bore her up like a mermaid, and all that time she chanted snatches of old tunes, like someone unaware of her own peril — or like a creature born to the water, native to that element. But it couldn't last long, before her garments, heavy with what they had drunk, pulled the poor girl from her melodious song down to muddy death.

**LAERTES:** So — then she is drowned?

**QUEEN GERTRUDE:** Drowned, drowned.

**LAERTES:** You've had too much water already, poor Ophelia, so I forbid myself tears. And yet — it's what we humans do; nature keeps her custom, whatever shame may say about it. When these tears are done, the woman in me will be out. — Goodbye, my lord. I have a speech of fire in me that wants to blaze — but this foolish weeping puts it out.

*[Exit.]*

**KING CLAUDIUS:** Let's follow him, Gertrude. What work I had to calm his rage! Now I'm afraid this will set it off again. So — let's follow.

*[Exeunt.]*
