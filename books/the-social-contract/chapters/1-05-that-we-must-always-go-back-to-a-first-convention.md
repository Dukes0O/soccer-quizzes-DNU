# Book I, Chapter V — That We Must Always Go Back to a First Convention

> Jean-Jacques Rousseau, *The Social Contract* (1762), trans. G. D. H. Cole · Book I, Chapter V
>
> [← I.IV Slavery](1-04-slavery.md) · [Book index](../README.md) · [I.VI The Social Compact →](1-06-the-social-compact.md)

## Overview

This short pivot chapter closes Rousseau's demolition of false foundations for political authority and sets up the constructive argument to come. Even if force, conquest, or voluntary enslavement could bind individuals to a master — claims he has just refuted — none of that would create a genuine political society. A conqueror with millions of subjects has only an *aggregation* of separate people held together by his private interest, not an *association* united by a public good; the proof is that his "empire" scatters the moment he dies.

Rousseau then turns Grotius's own argument against him: if a people can "give itself to a king," it must already *be* a people before the gift — and becoming a people is itself a civil act requiring public deliberation. That prior act, not any later choice of ruler, is the true foundation of society. Even the rule that a majority vote binds the minority can only rest on an earlier, unanimous agreement. So every legitimate polity traces back to a first convention — the social compact that the next chapter will finally examine.

## Key Concepts

- **Aggregation vs. association** — A crowd of individuals subdued by one master is a mere aggregation, a pile of separate units; an association is a genuine union with a shared public good and a collective existence. Only the latter is a people.
- **The first convention** — The founding agreement by which a multitude constitutes itself as a people. It necessarily precedes any act (like choosing a king) that the people performs, and it is the real basis of society.
- **Majority rule presupposes unanimity** — The rule that the minority must accept the majority's choice is itself a conventional rule, and it can only have been established by a unanimous agreement at least once.
- **Body politic** — The collective political body formed by association; Rousseau's point here is that a master and his slaves, however numerous, never form one.

## Archaic Terms

Words and usages that have fallen out of use or shifted meaning (in Cole's translation or Rousseau's terminology).

## Modern Translation

Even if I granted everything I have just been refuting, the champions of despotism would be no better off. There will always be a great difference between subduing a multitude and governing a society. Suppose scattered individuals, however many of them there were, were enslaved one after another by a single man: I would still see nothing there but a master and his slaves — certainly not a people and its ruler. It would be what you might call an aggregation, but not an association; there is still no public good and no body politic. That man, even if he enslaved half the world, would remain nothing but a private individual; his interest, standing apart from everyone else's, would still be a purely private interest. And when this same man dies, his empire is left scattered and without unity, just as an oak collapses into a heap of ashes once the fire has consumed it.

A people, says Grotius, can give itself to a king. On Grotius's own account, then, a people is already a people before it makes the gift. The gift itself is a civil act; it presupposes public deliberation. So before examining the act by which a people gives itself to a king, we would do well to examine the act by which it became a people in the first place. That act necessarily comes before the other, and it is the true foundation of society.

Indeed, if there were no prior convention, then — unless the election were unanimous — what would obligate the minority to submit to the choice of the majority? By what right do a hundred men who want a master vote on behalf of ten who do not? The rule of majority voting is itself something established by convention, and it presupposes unanimity on at least one occasion.
