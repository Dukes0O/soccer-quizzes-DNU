# The Social Contract (Books I–II) — Modern English Edition

**Jean-Jacques Rousseau, 1762** · from the G. D. H. Cole translation (1913), rendered into modern English, 2026

A chapter-by-chapter modern-English rendering of Books I and II of Rousseau's
*The Social Contract; or, Principles of Political Right*, produced for readers
working through the Great Books of Western Thought. This is a translation, not
an abridgment: every paragraph is rendered in full, and Rousseau's own
footnotes are inlined where he placed them. Each chapter file contains an
**Overview**, **Key Concepts**, an **Archaic Terms** glossary, and the complete
**Modern Translation**.

**Source:** [Project Gutenberg #46333](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/46333)
(the Cole translation, via the Standard Ebooks corrected text). The
unmodernized chapter texts are preserved under [`source/`](source/). The
machine-readable index is [`book.json`](book.json).

## Why this book matters

"Man is born free; and everywhere he is in chains." Rousseau's answer to how
political authority can be legitimate — only through a social compact in which
each person, by giving himself to all, obeys only the general will and remains
as free as before — is the hinge between Locke's consent-based liberalism and
modern democratic (and revolutionary) thought. Book I derives the compact and
demolishes rival foundations (paternal authority, force, slavery, conquest);
Book II develops sovereignty, the general will, law, and the extraordinary
figure of the Legislator. Read against Locke's *Second Treatise*: both ground
government in consent, but where Locke's sovereign people delegate to a
limited government and retain natural rights, Rousseau's sovereign is the
assembled people itself, and rights are transformed, not merely protected.

## Translation notes

Rousseau wrote in French; this edition modernizes the standard public-domain
English translation by G. D. H. Cole (1913). The technical vocabulary that is
still standard in political philosophy is retained — *general will*,
*sovereign*, *social compact*, *body politic*, *alienate* (in the
giving-up-rights sense), *the Legislator* — and Rousseau's famous sentences
are kept verbatim. Rousseau's own footnotes appear inline as marked quotes.
Books III–IV are not part of this edition (the reading plan covers Books I–II).
